# Contractor Outreach Kit

Who we're after: Arizona contractors who sell and build outdoor projects but can't produce 3D renders, HOA submittals, spec sheets or measured build plans in-house.

The offer: we draw it, spec it and get it approved; they build it. The client stays theirs. Referrals go both ways.

Rule: nothing gets sent without Tone's yes. Claude drafts; Tone approves each message or batch.

---

## 1. Who to target (best first)

1. **Pool builders.** Every pool job needs a backyard around it, and HOAs review it. Small and mid-size builders rarely have a designer.
2. **Hardscape / landscape contractors** (Arizona ROC classifications R-21, C-21 or CR-21 Hardscaping and Irrigation Systems). Look them up on the ROC license search at azroc.gov and confirm the license is active.
3. **Patio cover and ramada builders.**
4. **Outdoor kitchen and BBQ island builders.**
5. **General contractors** doing backyard remodels.

Where to find them:
- Google Maps searches: "pool builder Gilbert", "pavers Mesa", "landscape contractor Queen Creek". Note who has weak or no project photos. They need us most.
- Houzz, Yelp, Nextdoor pro listings.
- Facebook groups for Arizona contractors and landscapers.
- Supply yards (stone yards, nurseries, paver suppliers). Leave a printed card and ask the counter staff who's busy.
- Local HOA management companies: they see which contractors get rejected for incomplete submittals.

Build a simple sheet: company, owner name, city, trade, ROC #, website, phone, email, one specific observation, status.

## 2. Cold email sequence

Before sending at volume: send from a separate domain or inbox (not the main business address), set up SPF/DKIM/DMARC, verify every address, start at 20-30 a day, turn off open tracking. Every email needs an opt-out line and a postal address (CAN-SPAM law).

The `{observation}` line must be something real and specific from their website, Google profile or Instagram. If you can delete it and the email still works, it isn't specific enough.

## 3. Phone script (30 seconds)

> Hi, is this {name}? This is {your name} with Stone & Rose. We're a design studio here in the Valley. We do 3D renders, HOA submittals and measured build plans for contractors, so you can show clients the finished yard when you bid. You keep the client, we just do the drawings. Do you ever lose bids because the client can't picture it, or get stuck in HOA review?

If yes: "Can I text you a couple of sample renders? What's the best number?"
If no: "Fair enough. Mind if I text you my number in case a job comes up?"

## 6. Handling replies

| Reply | Response |
|---|---|
| Interested | Within the hour: thank them, send 2 sample renders, offer two specific call times. |
| "How much?" | Ask the yard size and what they need (renders only, or full package), then quote. Mention ongoing rates for regular partners. |
| "We do our own drawings" | "Makes sense. If you ever get backed up or need a render for a big bid, we're here." Stop. |
| "Not now" | Thank them, ask if you can check back in 3 months. Note the date. |
| Wrong person | Ask who handles design or bids. |
| Unsubscribe | Remove immediately, log it, never contact again. |

## 7. Keeping partners (the recurring part)

- One point of contact who learns how each partner likes their plans drawn.
- Send every homeowner who needs a builder to a partner, and tell the partner who sent them.
- Quick check-in text every month: "Anything coming up I can draw for you?"
- Track jobs each partner sends you and jobs you send them. Keep it balanced.
- Consider a simple partner agreement covering turnaround time, revisions included, rates, and who owns the client.
