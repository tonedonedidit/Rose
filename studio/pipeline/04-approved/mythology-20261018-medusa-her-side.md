They carved my face on shields to frighten you. Ask me why.
This is a legend from ancient Greece, as the Roman poet Ovid and others told it.
I am Medusa. Before the snakes, I was a woman. The one mortal among my sisters.
Ovid says I was famous for my beauty. My hair most of all.
Then the sea god Neptune found me in the temple of Minerva. He assaulted me there, in her sacred place.
You would think the goddess punished him.
She did not. She hid her eyes behind her shield.
But that wasn't the cruelest part.
She turned on me.
My hair became serpents. My gaze became a curse. Whoever met it turned to stone.
I lived at the edge of the world. A hero came hunting anyway.
Perseus. A polished shield to see me without looking. Winged sandals from the nymphs.
He took my head while I slept.
And even then, Minerva wasn't finished. She set my face on her own shield.
So when you see my face on a shield,
remember who was punished.
And ask me why.
