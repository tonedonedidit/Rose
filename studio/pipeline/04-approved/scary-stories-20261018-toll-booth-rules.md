Rule one: never answer the booth phone after two a.m.
I found the rules taped under the register on my first night at the toll booth. Handwritten. Six of them.
Rule two: the road is empty from two to four. If a car stops, take the toll. Don't look at the passengers.
Rule three: if the car has no driver, take the toll anyway.
Rule four: the fog comes at 3:10. Keep your window shut until it lifts.
Six nights, I followed every rule.
But on the seventh night, the rules stopped protecting me.
The fog came at 3:10. A car rolled out of it. A family inside, all facing forward.
My window was open. I don't remember opening it.
The mother held out a coin.
Then the booth phone rang.
I didn't answer. Rule one.
It rang eleven times. Then my cell buzzed. A text, from the booth's number.
"Read rule seven."
There was no rule seven. Then I flipped the paper over.
It was in my handwriting.
"Don't take the coin. You took it last time."
My hand was already closed around it.
