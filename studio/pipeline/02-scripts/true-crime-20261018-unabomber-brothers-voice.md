**Narration:**

For seventeen years, the FBI hunted a man nobody had seen.
Between 1978 and 1995, he mailed or planted sixteen bombs. Three people were killed. Agents called him the Unabomber.
He left almost no trace. His devices were handmade, from scrap wood and metal.
So how do you catch someone who leaves nothing behind?
In 1995, he made an offer. Print my manifesto, and the bombing stops.
Thirty-five thousand words.
On September 19th, The Washington Post published it, with The New York Times sharing the cost.
But that wasn't the part that cracked the case.
One reader felt sick.
His name was David Kaczynski. The ideas sounded familiar. So did the phrasing.
It sounded like his older brother, Ted.
David compared the text to old family letters. Through a lawyer, he went to the FBI.
On April 3rd, 1996, agents arrived at a small cabin outside Lincoln, Montana.
Inside: bomb components, journals, and a handwritten draft of the manifesto.
In January 1998, Ted Kaczynski pleaded guilty. Life without parole.
The clue wasn't a fingerprint.
It was his own voice, on paper. The one trace he couldn't hide.
